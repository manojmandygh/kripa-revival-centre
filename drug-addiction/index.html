Drug addiction

Recovery from drugs is possible.

A confidential conversation is the first step. We answer 24/7.

Understanding the problem

Drug dependence is an illness, not a failing.

Drugs are often first used to feel good, to cope, or to be part of a group. Dependence can follow quietly, and it rarely yields to willpower alone.

Kripa treats drug addiction as a whole-person illness - caring for the body through medical support, the mind through counselling and education, and the relationships around each person through family involvement.

When to reach out

Signs to watch for.

  • Feelings of hopelessness
  • Loss of interest in activities once pleasurable
  • Persistent sad, anxious, or empty feelings
  • Irritability and restlessness
  • Fatigue and decreased energy
  • Feelings of guilt, worthlessness, or helplessness
  • Difficulty concentrating and making decisions
  • Insomnia, early-morning wakefulness, or excessive sleeping
  • Thoughts of suicide

How Kripa helps

Structure, safety and time.

Withdrawal and stabilisation are medically supervised. Recovery then follows a structured four-month programme: individual and group therapy, 12-Step work of AA/NA, relapse prevention planning, family sessions and a steady daily routine.

What we treat

Whatever has taken hold, there is a way back.

Dependence looks different from person to person. Kripa's drug de-addiction track helps people step away from:

  • Benzodiazepines and other sleeping-pill dependence
  • Cannabis
  • Cocaine
  • Codeine
  • Heroin and other opiates
  • Mephedrone
  • Prescription medicines used beyond their prescription
  • Alcohol - through our dedicated alcohol de-addiction programme

Not sure whether what you are seeing is addiction? Call us. That first conversation costs nothing and stays confidential.
